# H22B: a second, independent census of H22 at n = 22

## Verdict

**H22 holds at n = 22 by a second, independent method.** geng gives 2,806,490 isomorphism classes
of connected bipartite 4-regular graphs on 22 vertices. 2,806,335 of them have a Hamilton
decomposition, and two programs check each certificate. The other 155 have a 2-edge cut, so they
cannot be HD. No graph is left over: none lacks a 2-edge cut and also lacks a decomposition.

The counts match the original exactly:

- 2U - S = 5,582,592 color-preserving classes.
- 2,806,335 HD graphs give 5,582,356 color-preserving classes.
- 155 graphs with a 2-edge cut give 236 color-preserving classes.
- Up to isomorphism, the 155 are exactly the original's 236 non-HD graphs (labelg).

Rung (b), a finite census. H22 now has two independent methods at every n <= 22.

## 1. How the original was done

Sources: `wave3/pairs/run_q4a11.sh`, `wave3/pairs/LOG.md` (11:05), `SCOUT.md` §2.3 and `REVIEW.md`.

- **Generator.** `genbg -q -c -d4:4 -D4:4 11 11 44:44 {res}/24`, run as 24 shards, three at a time. genbg
  keeps the two color classes apart, so a graph with no side-exchanging automorphism is counted twice.
  The shard summaries sum to 5,582,592 graphs, 5,582,356 HD and 0 unknown (I re-summed
  `data/q4a11/err_*.txt`).
- **HD decider.** `ptool H` (`wave3/pairs/ptool.c`). It enumerates Hamilton cycles depth-first from
  vertex 0 and, for each one, searches for a Hamilton cycle in the 2-regular remainder. Its randomized
  fallback runs only under a step limit, and none was set. It prints the 236 non-HD graphs, which are in
  `wave3/pairs/data/q4bip_a11_nonhd.g6`.
- **2-edge cuts.** `cuts.c` ran on those 236 graphs only (every T containing vertex 0, in Gray-code
  order).
- **Referee.** The referee used geng with `review/code/hd.c` (Hamilton-cycle DFS, then a connectivity
  check on the complement) and networkx `swapcount.py`, for n <= 20 only. At n = 22 only the shard
  summaries were read.

The brief suggested genbg for this lane, but genbg is the original's generator. This lane uses
**geng** (a different generation algorithm), and all of its decision code is new. The count
conversion therefore runs the other way from the brief: geng counts uncolored classes, so
2U - S gives the color-preserving count, where S is the number of classes with a side-exchanging
automorphism.

## 2. Method of this lane

| Stage | Original | This lane |
|---|---|---|
| Generation | genbg, color classes fixed | `geng -c -b -d4 -D4 22 44:44 res/2000`, uncolored classes |
| 2-edge cuts | cuts.c, on the 236 non-HD graphs only | `hdq` on every graph, by two methods that must agree: a bridge of G - e (DFS lowpoints) for every edge e, and a brute force over all sets of at most 2 edges (`-B`) |
| HD decision | ptool H: enumerate Hamilton cycles, test the remainder | `hdq`: a search over edge 2-colorings with 2 red and 2 blue edges at every vertex (details below) |
| Certificates | none kept | HD graph: the red edge set, re-checked by a separate routine in `hdq` and again by `verify_certs.py` (own Python graph6 parser). 2-cut graph: the two cut edges, re-checked by `verify_certs.py` |
| Second decider | none | `sat_hd.py`: PySAT CaDiCaL 1.5.3, exactly 2 red edges per vertex, lazy cut clauses (some edge leaving each red cycle is red; some edge leaving each blue cycle is blue) |
| Count conversion | not needed | `hdq` swap test: an individualization-refinement search for an automorphism exchanging the sides, then 2U - S. Cross-checked against labelg 2-colored canonical forms on every graph |

The `hdq` search tracks the ends of the red and blue paths, so no cycle of either color closes
before it has 22 edges. It forces colors where it can: a vertex with 2 edges of one color gets the
other color on its remaining edges, and an edge joining the two ends of a short path gets the other
color. It prunes a branch when the red-or-uncolored graph or the blue-or-uncolored graph stops being
connected and bridgeless.

**Why a 2-edge cut rules out HD.** A Hamilton cycle crosses every edge cut at least twice, so two
edge-disjoint Hamilton cycles need at least 4 edges in every cut. H22 therefore holds exactly when
every graph gets one of two certificates: a decomposition or a 2-edge cut. A graph with neither
would be an exception (line type X) and would be re-decided by SAT. There were none.

**Independence of the code.** `hdq.c` shares no code with ptool.c, cuts.c or hd.c, and its HD
search is a different algorithm: it does not enumerate Hamilton cycles. I read ptool.c's Hamilton
search and the first 30 lines of hd.c only to describe them here.

## 3. Commands

    /usr/bin/clang -O2 -Wall -Wextra -o code/hdq code/hdq.c         # Apple clang 21.0.0
    # one shard, res = 0..1999 (code/run_shard.sh; it checks geng's >Z count = hdq count = lines)
    nice -n 10 geng -c -b -d4 -D4 22 44:44 RES/2000 | nice -n 10 code/hdq -B
    nohup code/driver.sh 22 2000 10 <abs>/data/n22 > logs/driver_n22.log 2>&1 &   # restart-safe
    python3 code/collect.py data/n22 2000
    nohup code/final_checks.sh > logs/final_checks.log 2>&1 &

`final_checks.sh` runs `verify_certs.py` on all lines, `sat_hd.py` on every 2-cut line and on every
1000th HD line, a labelg duplicate check, the labelg comparison with the original's 236 graphs, and
the labelg cross-check of the swap flags. geng is `~/.cache/erdos585/nauty2_9_3/geng` (nauty 2.9.3).

## 7. Cost

- Pilot: geng on 5 of 2000 slices, 14.4 to 17.9 CPU seconds each, projecting about 9 CPU-hours.
- Run: geng's own CPU total is 31,933.6 s (8.87 CPU-hours), on 2000 shards, 10 at a time at nice 10.
  Wall time was 79 minutes (21:31 to 22:50 EDT) while other jobs held the load average between about
  34 and 194.
- `hdq` takes about 30 µs per graph including the brute-force cut check, about 2 CPU-minutes in all.
- Final checks took 7 minutes wall.
- Total: under 9.5 core-hours, against a budget of 25.

## 8. What this shows, and what it shares with the original

**Different from the original:**

- the generator program and its algorithm (geng, not genbg);
- the 2-cut test (run on every graph, by two methods);
- the HD decider (algorithm and code);
- the second decider (SAT);
- the count conversion and the certificate checker.

**Shared with the original:**

- geng and genbg are both nauty programs and use nauty's canonical labeling, so a fault common to
  that core could in principle affect both enumerations. labelg is also nauty; this lane uses it
  only for cross-checks (duplicates, swap flags, comparison with the original's list).
- The graph6 format and the machine.

**Generator completeness.** Neither run proves that its generator is complete. The evidence is that
two different generators agree exactly:

- 2U - S = 5,582,592 at n = 22, and the same agreement at every n from 10 to 20;
- the color-preserving non-HD counts agree;
- the sets of 2-cut classes agree.

geng's res/mod split is taken from nauty's documentation. The count match and the absence of
duplicates support it.
